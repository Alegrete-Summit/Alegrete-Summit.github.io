Startups born in ecosystems that grew with AI

From the UFSM incubator to Porto Digital (Recife's tech park), from AgTech Valley to Tecnopuc: 18 Brazilian startups that came out of universities, incubators and technology parks and made artificial intelligence the center of their business. Each case shows its origin in the ecosystem and figures with sources.

Cowmed: the AI that listens to the herd

It started as a student project at UFSM, in Santa Maria, was incubated at Pulsar, and today monitors 100,000 animals with collars and its own AI, in Brazil, Paraguay, Uruguay and Mexico.

Criteria

How we chose

1 · Origin

Born in or passed through a Brazilian ecosystem: an incubator, technology park, university spin-off or a program linked to a hub. With a source.

2 · AI at the core

Artificial intelligence, machine learning or computer vision is a central part of the product, not an add-on.

3 · Results

Scale, investment, acquisition or measurable impact. Figures reported only by the company appear as a company figure.

Agribusiness · dairy farming

Cowmed

Santa Maria · RS

Ecosystem
A UFSM student project, incubated at and graduated from Pulsar, the university's technology incubator.
How it uses AI
A sensor collar takes 25 readings per second of rumination, feeding, activity and panting. In the cloud, the company's own AI generates alerts for heat (estrus), illness and heat stress.
Results
  • 2022: R$5.9 million from 547 investors, a record for equity crowdfunding in Brazil; 35,000 cows on more than 400 farms.
  • 2026: 100,000 animals monitored; revenue of about R$18 million in 2025 (company figure); Paraguay, Uruguay and Mexico.

For the Pampa: a university incubator in inland Rio Grande do Sul, focused on the region's livestock, produced an AI company that exports.

Sources: UFSM · Forbes, 2022 · CNN Brasil, 2026

Health · hospital pharmacy

NoHarm.ai

Porto Alegre · RS

Ecosystem
Born from Henrique Dias's PhD in Computer Science at PUCRS, together with pharmacist Ana Helena Ulbrich. It is part of Tecnopuc and of Navi, the park's AI hub.
How it uses AI
Two models analyze hospital prescriptions: one ranks those that deviate from the norm, the other identifies critical patients. The pharmacist reviews the highest-risk ones first.
Results
  • 2021: 13 hospitals, about 15,000 lives.
  • 2025: more than 200 hospitals, clinics and facilities in 3 states and more than 5 million prescriptions per month (company figure). Free for SUS (Brazil's public health system).
  • Ana Helena Ulbrich was named to the 2025 TIME100 AI list.

For the Pampa: a PhD thesis and a problem from inside the hospital can become a service that private hospitals pay for and SUS gets for free.

Sources: PUCRS, 2021 · Fast Company Brasil, 2025 · Jornal do Comércio, 2025

Health · clinic management

GestãoDS

Santa Maria · RS

Ecosystem
Started at UFSM, while the founders were still Business Administration students, and was incubated at Pulsar.
How it uses AI
AI transcription of consultations, which organizes and classifies the notes, and patient scheduling via chat. In 2025, an R$2.5 million contract with CESAR, with support from Embrapii (Brazil's industrial research and innovation agency), to build AI agents for healthcare.
Results
  • 2025: more than 15,000 active users and a valuation of R$60 million (company figures).

For the Pampa: AI can come in later, through partners such as CESAR and Embrapii. What matters is already having customers and data.

Sources: UFSM · Baguete, 2025

Energy · computer vision

Pix Force

Porto Alegre · RS

Ecosystem
Originated in UFRGS labs and the Zenit incubator; resident at Instituto Caldeira and accelerated by Oxygea Labs.
How it uses AI
Computer vision with drones and cameras to inspect power grids, check safety equipment, control quality and read documents.
Results
  • 2023: solution adopted by Cemig to monitor its power grid.
  • More than 100 projects for companies such as Vibra, Comgás and Neoenergia (company figure).
  • 2024: an office in Houston, in the US, even after losing about 70% of its hardware in the Rio Grande do Sul floods.

For the Pampa: computer vision applied to an infrastructure problem takes a startup from Rio Grande do Sul to other states and other countries.

Sources: Extra Classe, 2022 · Baguete, 2023 · Startups.com.br, 2024

Credit · risk and fraud

Finor

Porto Alegre · RS

Ecosystem
Academic spin-off: both founders have been Economics professors at UFRGS for more than ten years.
How it uses AI
Credit risk models, fraud detection and portfolio optimization.
Results
  • Clients such as Bradesco, unico, Edenred and the World Bank's IFC; projects in Brazil and the US.
  • October 2025: acquired by Artefact, a French AI consultancy valued at US$1 billion.

For the Pampa: quantitative research by professors at a university in Rio Grande do Sul can attract global buyers.

Source: Baguete, 2025

Agribusiness · soil fertility

InCeres

Piracicaba · SP

Ecosystem
A company from ESALQTec, the Esalq/USP incubator, in AgTech Valley; supported by FAPESP PIPE (São Paulo state small-business research grants) and Finep (Brazil's innovation funding agency).
How it uses AI
Models that combine soil analyses, climate, crop and satellite imagery to predict the fertility of each field plot and recommend fertilization.
Results
  • 2016: R$2.5 million from SP Ventures.
  • August 2024: acquired by Sweden's Husqvarna. The negotiation began at AgTech Garage, in Piracicaba.

For the Pampa: being in the ecosystem also brings buyers closer. InCeres's exit began in the hallways of a hub.

Sources: Esalq · FAPESP · The AgriBiz, 2024

Agribusiness · weeds

Cromai

Piracicaba · SP

Ecosystem
Resident at Pulse, Raízen's innovation hub in AgTech Valley (a corporate program linked to the ecosystem).
How it uses AI
Proprietary models, trained on more than 100 million drone and satellite images, locate weeds and generate the map for targeted spraying. AI-powered sensors measure impurities in the sugarcane arriving at the mills.
Results
  • 2022: 300,000 hectares of sugarcane and a Series A of about R$15 million.
  • 2024: investment led by Totvs; 45 mills, 9 of the 10 largest sugarcane groups (company figure).

For the Pampa: solve a large regional customer's pain point first, then take the solution to other crops.

Sources: PwC, 2022 · Brazil Journal, 2024

Agribusiness · climate and irrigation

Agrosmart

Itajubá · MG / Campinas · SP

Ecosystem
A company from ESALQTec that received investment from SP Ventures; went through Startup Brasil and a Google acceleration program.
How it uses AI
Machine learning on data from soil sensors, drones, satellites and weather to recommend planting, irrigation and crop management.
Results
  • 2019: Series A of US$5.8 million.
  • 2021: acquired Argentina's BoosterAgro; together, more than 48 million hectares in 9 countries.

For the Pampa: the founder came from an inland university and was not an agronomist. Incubator, accelerator and venture capital combined made the scale possible.

Sources: Esalq · AgFunderNews, 2021

Agribusiness and climate · soil carbon

Agrorobótica

São Carlos · SP

Ecosystem
Born from a partnership with Embrapa Instrumentação, part of Embrapa (Brazil's agricultural research corporation), with support from Embrapa's Pontes para Inovação program and Embrapii/USP. Embrapa receives part of the royalties.
How it uses AI
Laser spectroscopy with machine learning models that convert the spectrum into more than 20 soil parameters, including carbon, in about 20 seconds. Methodology approved by Verra in 2022.
Results
  • April 2026: R$10.8 million from VOX Capital and Banco do Brasil's corporate fund.
  • More than 1 million hectares analyzed in 18 states; clients such as Bunge, Amaggi, SLC and Syngenta (company figure).

For the Pampa: licensing Embrapa technology is a shortcut to a deep tech company. Soil carbon in native grasslands is a path to explore with Embrapa Pecuária Sul.

Sources: AgFeed · The AgriBiz, 2026

Forestry · inventory

Treevia

São José dos Campos · SP

Ecosystem
Incubated at Nexus, in the São José dos Campos Technology Park, with FAPESP PIPE and acceleration from Samsung with Anprotec.
How it uses AI
Sensors continuously measure tree diameter; machine learning turns the data into a remote forest inventory.
Results
  • 2024: project with Melhoramentos on more than 7,000 hectares, with an expected 18% reduction in inventory costs.

For the Pampa: forestry in Rio Grande do Sul has the same problem. A small-business research grant plus a technology park reduces the technical risk.

Sources: Agência FAPESP · Agrolink, 2024

Data · health and credit

Kunumi

Belo Horizonte · MG

Ecosystem
Spin-off of the UFMG Computer Science Department, in the BH-Tec park. UFMG shares in the results and transferred intellectual property.
How it uses AI
Proprietary neural networks. For Hospital Sírio-Libanês, a model using ICU data predicted mortality 27% better than the SAPS 3 score; for banks, credit models.
Results
  • May 2024: acquired by Bradesco (amount not disclosed).
  • According to the bank, gains of at least R$250 million and response time for wholesale credit requests falling from 10 to 3.3 days (buyer's figure, 2026).

For the Pampa: when the university is a partner in the spin-off, success also flows back to research.

Sources: UFMG, 2016 · Diário do Comércio, 2024 · Let's Money, 2026

Health · cancer

Huna

Belo Horizonte · MG

Ecosystem
The idea emerged in the UFMG AI lab, where co-founder Daniella Castro worked.
How it uses AI
Finds cancer risk patterns in routine blood counts the patient has already had. It is screening; it does not replace mammography.
Results
  • Model trained on data from more than 5 million patients; in a study of about 400,000 women, it flagged 2,861 at-risk cases.
  • 2025: studies presented at the ASCO meeting and a US$1.5 million funding round.

For the Pampa: data that SUS already produces, such as lab tests, can become a product without requiring new tests.

Sources: Exame, 2025 · Futuro da Saúde, 2025

Government · language

NeuralMind

Campinas · SP

Ecosystem
At the Unicamp Science and Technology Park; founded by Roberto Lotufo, a Unicamp professor, and Patrícia Tavares, former director of Inova Unicamp.
How it uses AI
Created BERTimbau, a Portuguese language model, and tools that read documents and extract data from them. For the TCU (Federal Court of Accounts), it developed generative AI that helps auditors analyze complaints about public procurement.
Results
  • 2022: won the TCU call for proposals against 18 other bids, with support from FAPESP PIPE.

For the Pampa: technology procurement by public agencies is a path for university teams to create GovTechs.

Sources: Parque Unicamp · Agência FAPESP

Credit · risk

Neurotech

Recife · PE

Ecosystem
Born at the UFPE Informatics Center and incubated at CESAR, in Porto Digital.
How it uses AI
Neural networks and machine learning for credit, fraud and risk decisions since the early 2000s.
Results
  • 2022: acquired by B3 (Brazil's stock exchange) for R$620 million, potentially reaching about R$1.1 billion if targets are met; more than 320 employees.
  • 2026: B3 sets up an operation in Porto Digital with about 350 professionals from Neurotech.

For the Pampa: a university plus an incubator, far from the Rio–São Paulo axis, built a R$1 billion AI company. It took 20 years of focus on one problem.

Sources: B3, 2022 · Forbes, 2022 · Movimento Econômico, 2026

Fraud · digital identity

Incognia

Recife · PE and US

Ecosystem
Started as a project by UFPE Computer Science students (then called In Loco), in the Porto Digital ecosystem.
How it uses AI
Location and device signals feed models that calculate fraud risk; in 2026, it launched AI agent detection.
Results
  • 2019: US$20 million; 2022: Series A of US$15.5 million; 2024: Series B of US$31 million led by Bessemer.
  • Offices in San Jose, New York, São Paulo and Recife.

For the Pampa: a class project becomes a global company when it tackles a problem the whole world has.

Sources: Exame · Incognia, 2024

Industry · inspection

Mvisia

São Paulo · SP

Ecosystem
Founded in 2012 at the USP innovation and entrepreneurship center.
How it uses AI
Smart cameras and computer vision to inspect quality on the production line.
Results
  • 2020: WEG bought 51% and integrated the technology into its internet of things platform; it later agreed to buy the rest.

For the Pampa: not every success story is a unicorn. Being acquired by a major regional manufacturer is also a win.

Sources: Forbes, 2020 · Startupi

Industry · data

Aquarela Analytics

Florianópolis · SC

Ecosystem
Graduated from the MIDI Tecnológico incubator, run by Acate with Sebrae-SC; backed by Fundo Aeroespacial.
How it uses AI
Proprietary platform with AI models for inventory and supply chain planning.
Results
  • Has served Embraer since 2015, as well as Mercedes-Benz, Scania, Coca-Cola and Vivo.

For the Pampa: a large anchor customer can be worth more than venture capital.

Sources: Baguete · Startups.com.br, 2026

Logistics · routing

RoutEasy

São Paulo · SP

Ecosystem
Spin-off of USP's Escola Politécnica (engineering school): born from the routing algorithm in the founder's master's in Transportation Engineering.
How it uses AI
AI and optimization to plan routes, track deliveries and automate the last mile.
Results
  • 2021: R$5.8 million from Grupo Ultra's fund; more than 300 clients, such as DHL, Shopee and Magalu.
  • 2023: became part of the nstech group.

For the Pampa: a master's thesis is a product when it solves a daily operational pain point.

Sources: Startupi, 2021 · Exame, 2023

The pattern

What these cases have in common

A real problem

Undetected heat, wrong prescriptions, weeds in the field, credit fraud. None of them started with the technology.

Science behind it

PhDs, master's degrees, labs and Embrapa. Differentiated AI is born from research.

An ecosystem nearby

Incubators, parks, hubs and research funding reduced the risk in the early years.

Their own data

Collars, prescriptions, drone images: whoever has the data trains the best model.

Transparency

Who was left out, and why

Some of the best-known Brazilian AI startups were not included because we found no source confirming their origin in a Brazilian innovation ecosystem, or because AI is not the core of the product. That does not diminish their success; they simply do not fit the criteria of this page.

  • Tractian (industrial maintenance, a unicorn in 2026) and Solinftec (Solix robot): no confirmed link to a Brazilian incubator or park.
  • Laura (sepsis) and Letrus (essay grading): strong AI, but no confirmed origin in an ecosystem.
  • Aegro (UFRGS) and Farmcont (UFSM): origin confirmed, but AI is not central to the product.
