How AI affects the Pampa's sectors

Irrigated rice in the várzeas (lowland floodplains), soybeans, cattle ranching on native grassland, energy, cross-border trade, public services spread over long distances. Each sector of the Fronteira Oeste (Western Frontier region) and the Campanha has its own way of being transformed by artificial intelligence, and its own precautions to take.

The numbers below come from real trials and projects, with the source alongside. When a number comes from the manufacturer or the client itself, we say so. A result from somewhere else is a clue, not a promise: in the Pampa, it has to be measured again.

01

Irrigated rice and water

The crop that defines the landscape of the várzeas of Alegrete and the Fronteira Oeste depends on water, energy and careful management of the flood depth.

What changes

  • Irrigation decided with daily data from weather stations
  • Optical sorters at the rice mills that learn the colors of each lot and reject defective grains
  • Satellite monitoring of the crop throughout the season

Opportunity in the region

Water balance for each paddy, alerts for water shortages in reservoirs, pump sizing and lower energy costs for pumping.

Where to start

Data from the INMET weather station in Alegrete, flow measurements in the field and a well-guided AI assistant (see the sample prompt).

Watch out

A model calibrated for another crop or another soil gets it wrong on lowland Planosol (Planossolo). Validate it in the field plot before changing management.

36%less water per kg of coffee and 15% less energy in a pilot of AI-decided irrigation on 20 farms in Espírito Santo (company figures; Baguete, 2026).
02

Soybeans and grains

Soybeans have spread across the Campanha and the Fronteira Oeste. Inputs are the biggest cost, and that is exactly where AI strikes.

What changes

  • Spot spraying: cameras on the boom tell weeds from the crop and the nozzle opens only over the weed
  • Drones and deep learning to map weeds and generate the application map
  • Grain grading by image, in minutes

Opportunity in the region

Drone mapping services, application prescriptions, fast grading at grain intake and precision agriculture startups.

Where to start

Yield maps the combine harvester already produces, free satellite imagery and partnerships with agronomy and agricultural engineering programs.

Watch out

Official grading still requires an accredited grader. AI speeds things up; technical responsibility remains human.

76%less herbicide on average, measured by Iowa State in 5 soybean fields with computer-vision spraying (Iowa State, 2024). Embrapa is testing image-based soybean grading that cuts analysis from 90 to 4 minutes (Cultivar, 2026).
03

Livestock and native grassland

Beef cattle and sheep on native grassland are part of the Pampa's identity. Computer vision is starting to see what the ranch hand's eye already knew.

What changes

  • Identification of invasive plants, such as capim-annoni (an invasive African lovegrass), from photos
  • Image-based detection of ticks on cattle
  • Technical assistance by message, via text, audio or photo

Opportunity in the region

Pasture monitoring, weight estimation from images, traceability and certification of beef raised on native grassland.

Where to start

Geotagged photos taken on a phone, organized management records and closer ties with Unipampa and Embrapa Pecuária Sul.

Watch out

AI-based animal health diagnosis is screening. The decision and the prescription remain with the veterinarian.

88% and 94%accuracy in identifying capim-annoni in the field and ticks on cattle, in a Unipampa project with Embrapa Pecuária Sul and support from Nvidia (Gazeta do Povo, 2025). Research done here, in the Pampa.
04

The Pampa biome and the environment

The Pampa is the Brazilian biome that has lost the largest proportion of its native vegetation in recent decades. Without data, there is no way to protect it or to produce responsibly.

What changes

  • Annual land-use maps produced by machine learning over the entire satellite archive
  • A basis for the CAR (Rural Environmental Registry), licensing, expert assessments and rural credit
  • Monitoring of the sandy areas of southwestern Rio Grande do Sul, found in municipalities such as Alegrete and Manoel Viana

Opportunity in the region

Environmental services, conservation credits for native grassland, consultancies that check and correct automated maps.

Where to start

MapBiomas maps are free and cover every municipality since 1985.

Watch out

In the Pampa, grassland, forest and tree plantations are among the classes most often confused. The automated map needs someone who knows the land.

9.8 → 5.9million hectares of native grassland in the Pampa, from 1985 to 2024, according to MapBiomas, which classifies the images with machine learning (MapBiomas, 2025).
05

Energy

Abundant sun and wind, crops that pump water all summer long and extensive rural grids.

What changes

  • Forecasting solar and wind generation from weather data
  • Predictive maintenance of motors, pumps and transformers
  • Scheduling pumping for hours when energy is cheaper

Opportunity in the region

Local electrical engineering and solar energy companies can offer smart monitoring as a service.

Where to start

Per-equipment consumption meters and billing history: with these, an AI assistant can already point out waste.

Watch out

AI itself consumes energy. Use models sized to the problem.

15%less pumping energy in the same AI irrigation pilot cited under Rice and water (company figures). On the Palco Geek (Geek Stage), Raul Ledur Kuhn of EGD Energia tells the story of an energy company that went from the Fronteira Oeste to the whole country.
06

Public administration

Municipalities with large territories, scattered populations and lean teams.

What changes

  • Citizen services through digital channels, at any hour
  • Reading and triage of case files and documents
  • Indicators to decide where to invest

Opportunity in the region

Local GovTechs, inter-municipal consortia to share solutions, and open data that feeds startups.

Where to start

A high-demand service with clear rules, such as appointments, duplicate documents or frequently asked questions.

Watch out

Transparency, the LGPD (Brazil's data protection law) and the right to talk to a person. Decisions about citizens' rights cannot be automated.

4startups with solutions for the public sector (PALM, Bright Cities, Digital Brasil and Era Capital) presented their products to the Rio Grande do Sul delegation at IMATech, in Campinas, during the São Paulo Mission (IMATech, 2026).
07

Health

Long distances to specialists and overloaded primary care teams.

What changes

  • Scheduling, reminders and fewer missed appointments
  • Support for reading test results, always with a professional's report
  • Medical record summaries and queue management

Opportunity in the region

More efficient telehealth for the interior and solutions for local clinics and pharmacies.

Where to start

Administrative processes, where the gain is large and the clinical risk is low.

Watch out

Health data is sensitive. No diagnosis without clinical validation and a responsible professional.

—No number here, on purpose: in health, only what has been clinically validated in the population served counts.
08

Education

Schools, federal institutes and universities train the people who will build the next generation of solutions.

What changes

  • One-on-one tutoring, at each student's pace
  • Teachers with more time for what only a person can do
  • Real projects from an early age: Edtech, robotics, programming

Opportunity in the region

Edtech startups, like those competing in IFFar's Bye Bye Boss, and training girls in technology, as Technovation Gurias does.

Where to start

Teach people to ask good questions and to check the answers. It is the basic skill of the AI era.

Watch out

AI can do the work for the student. Assessments need to value the process, not just the product.

13teams present Edtech and traditional innovation ideas at Bye Bye Boss, on October 29, at the Summit.
09

Commerce, services and communication

Small businesses make up most of the region's urban economy, and they gain the most from an "expanded team."

What changes

  • Content, photos and videos for social media in less time
  • Customer service by message, with consistent answers
  • Inventory, finance and pricing management backed by data

Opportunity in the region

Local agencies and creators who master AI, better-communicated gaúcho cultural tourism, service in Spanish for customers from across the border.

Where to start

One repetitive task a week: captions, customer replies, a sales report.

Watch out

A personal touch is what brings customers back. AI helps, but it doesn't replace the relationship.

Oct 30On the Palco Geek, Júlia Rodrigues talks about AI, human creativity and marketing, and Leidria Moreira asks what makes a customer choose to come back.
10

Logistics and the border

Bordering Argentina and Uruguay, the region is a corridor for freight and trade between the countries.

What changes

  • Translation and preparation of documents in Portuguese and Spanish
  • Route planning and transport demand forecasting
  • Automated reading of invoices, bills of lading and forms

Opportunity in the region

Binational services, faster customs brokers and carriers, integration with neighboring innovation ecosystems.

Where to start

Digitize and standardize documents. Without organized data, there is no AI.

Watch out

Mistakes in customs documents are costly. Human review on every operation.

RSThe Caravana de Impacto RS 2026 mobilized businesses in the Fronteira Oeste and the Campanha with support from the ecosystems of Alegrete, Uruguaiana, Santana do Livramento and Bagé, among others.
11

Machinery and agro-industry

Tractors, combine harvesters, sprayers and rice mills: the machinery fleet is getting "smart" too.

What changes

  • Autonomous sprayers supervised remotely
  • In-cab voice assistants that work offline and cite the manual
  • Predictive maintenance with sensors

Opportunity in the region

Technicians and engineers who configure, maintain and supervise smart fleets. Dealerships and repair shops with new services.

Where to start

Make good use of the data the machines already record, and push for connectivity in rural areas.

Watch out

Machines without an operator require safety protocols and someone responsible for the fleet.

1 → 4autonomous sprayers controlled by a single operator with the Arbus 4000 JAV, from Brazilian manufacturer Jacto (Cultivar, 2026).
The pattern

What all sectors have in common

Local data

Without data from the Pampa, AI repeats what it learned somewhere else.

People who know the trade

The farmer, the civil servant, the shopkeeper: they are the ones who notice when the machine gets it wrong.

Connectivity

Internet access in rural areas and in schools is innovation infrastructure.
