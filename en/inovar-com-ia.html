How to innovate with AI

Start with the problem, not the tool. Innovating with artificial intelligence means turning a real need of the region into a solution that is tested, measured and owned by someone. AI is there to shorten the path, not to choose the destination.

Roadmap

Six steps, from idea to region

  1. Choose a real, measurable problem

    "We waste time answering the same customer questions," "we don't know how much water the crop uses per bag harvested." If you can't measure before and after, it isn't a problem yet; it's a wish.

  2. Bring together data and the knowledge of those who do the work

    Spreadsheets, photos, records, invoices, and above all the experience of people who do the job every day. AI learns from data; innovation learns from people.

  3. Prototype in days

    AI assistants, no-code tools and smart spreadsheets let you test the idea before investing. The first prototype should be simple enough to throw away.

  4. Test with users from the region

    The farmer, the civil servant, the store customer. Watch where the solution gets it wrong. Models trained outside the Pampa tend to be wrong with confidence.

  5. Measure and manage the risks

    Compare with the "before." Check privacy (LGPD, Brazil's data protection law), bias, costs and what happens when the AI gets it wrong. Decide who checks and who is accountable.

  6. Scale with partners

    PampaTec's incubator and acceleration program, Sebrae and the Local Innovation Agents, Inova RS, universities, credit unions and innovation funding calls. Nobody scales alone: innovating is building bridges.

By profile

Where you start

Entrepreneur and microbusiness owner

One task a week

Pick a repetitive task (captions, customer replies, quotes) and use an AI assistant to do it with you. In a month, you'll have won back hours and learned what the tool does well and badly.

Rural producer

Start with the data you already have

Combine harvester maps, input invoices, recorded rainfall, photos of the crop. Organize them, ask the AI, and check with your agronomist. MapBiomas maps and INMET data are free.

Public manager

A service with clear rules

Appointments, duplicate documents, frequently asked questions. Organized process first, AI second, always with human oversight and transparency toward citizens.

Student

A project with a real problem

Bring a challenge from a company or the city into your capstone project, the Ideathon or Bye Bye Boss. Use AI to learn faster, not to skip the learning.

Researcher and teacher

Local data, local model

The Pampa needs its own datasets: images of native grassland, várzeas (lowland floodplains), cattle, regional diseases. Publishing open data is one of the biggest contributions to the ecosystem.

Technology company

A real problem becomes a product

As in the Contact Pró case: listen to the need, build the solution and improve it with data. AI is part of the architecture, not the whole product.

In practice

Weak prompt, good prompt

The quality of the answer depends on the quality of the question. The same topic, rice irrigation, asked two ways of an AI assistant.

Weak prompt

How much water does it take to irrigate rice?

You get a generic answer with wide ranges ("8,000 to 12,000 m³ per hectare") and no indication of where the numbers come from. Good for conversation, not for decisions.

Good prompt

I'm an Agricultural Engineering student in Alegrete (RS, Brazil). I have a 50 ha flood-irrigated rice field on lowland floodplain soil (Planosol), and I want to size the pump for January, at peak demand. Estimate the continuous flow rate, in L/s per hectare and in total. Show the components in a table, say where each value comes from, tell me what I need to measure in the field to confirm it, and don't make up numbers: when something is an estimate, say so. Answer in 250 words or fewer.

You get an estimate of about 1.3 L/s per hectare (67 L/s for 50 ha), with each component flagged as an estimate and a list of what to measure: evapotranspiration at the INMET weather station in Alegrete, percolation with an infiltrometer, and losses through the levees and canals. The math checks out.

Context

Who you are, where you are, what the situation is.

Task

Exactly what you want, with units and timeframe.

Format

Table, list, maximum length.

Honesty

Ask for sources and for estimates to be flagged.

Responsible use

Checklist before using the answer

  • A person has reviewed the result and takes responsibility for it.
  • Numbers, laws, regulations and quotes have been checked against the original source.
  • Personal data of customers, citizens or patients has not been pasted into tools without privacy guarantees.
  • The solution has been tested with data and users from the region.
  • People being served know there is AI in the process and can talk to a person.
  • There is a plan for when the AI gets it wrong.
  • The cost (money, energy, vendor dependence) is justified by the benefit.
For the Ideathon

Ten Pampa challenges to solve with AI

  1. Alert the farmer when the water depth in the rice paddy moves out of the ideal range.
  2. Identify capim-annoni (an invasive African lovegrass) in phone photos and map the outbreaks on the property.
  3. Answer citizens' most frequent questions about municipal services, with handoff to a person.
  4. Translate and standardize trade documents with Argentina and Uruguay.
  5. Help small retailers plan inventory based on sales.
  6. A math and reading tutor for students at rural schools.
  7. Track the recovery of native grassland and sandy areas with satellite images.
  8. Reduce missed medical appointments with reminders and automatic rescheduling.
  9. Promote the region's tourism and gaúcho culture in Portuguese, Spanish and English.
  10. Connect university research with companies that need it.
